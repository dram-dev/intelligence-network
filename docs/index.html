Sign up, step by step

The network lives inside a free messaging app. If you can send a text message, you can use it — nothing to pay, no account to create here.

Open the chat
1 · GET TELEGRAM

Telegram is a free messaging app, like WhatsApp or iMessage. Install it and sign up with your phone number.

iPhone & iPad Android Computer

Already have Telegram? Go straight to step 2.

2 · OPEN OUR CHAT

Press Open the chat above. A normal chat window opens with @intelligence_network_bot.

Or open Telegram, tap the search box at the top, type that name and pick it from the list. You are writing to a program, not a person: it replies straight away, day or night.

3 · SAY HELLO

Press the blue START button at the bottom of the chat. If you don't see one, type /join and send it.

A message that starts with a slash is an instruction rather than a sentence. Send /help whenever you want the full list.

4 · SAY WHERE YOU ARE

Send your ZIP code like this: /home 62704. Your reports and your alerts both use it.

On a phone you can instead tap the paperclip, choose Location, then Send My Current Location. Once is enough — we keep the place, not a trail.

5 · REPORT WHAT YOU SEE

Write it the short way and send it: rain 1.2in, hail quarter, trees down. The bot repeats back what it understood.

Other people see a handle like s-4f2a1 and your ZIP code — never your name, your number or your exact address.

Choose what gets sent to you

Answer three questions, then tap Open in Telegram and press Start: you're joined and subscribed in one step. Or copy the message and send it in the chat yourself. Do it again for anything else you want.

How trust works

        
Every reading is compared with readings of the same metric nearby, inside the metric's distance and time window: other people, weather stations, NWS storm reports and warnings, and NOAA's radar estimates of rain and hail (MRMS). Agreement corroborates both readings. A sensor's trust, kept separately for each topic, is

        
trust = (K·prior + agreements) / (K + agreements + disagreements)
K = 4 · prior = 0.5 · trusted ≥ 0.8 · half-life 180 days

        
Old outcomes fade. Radar counts half as much as an official report. Two people under one roof, or two who always agree, count as one witness. New sensors show "new" instead of a number that looks exact; after that, the board shades an 80% range. Nobody starts trusted; nobody is condemned by one odd reading.

Pull the network's own snapshot

      
The page is built from thirteen JSON files that anyone may fetch and reuse: data/network.json, counties.json, events.json, alerts.json, storms.json, reports.json, gauges.json, activity.json, graph.json, leaderboard.json, digests.json, topics.json, sources.json. Same anonymisation as the page.

      
For other systems: the week's verified events as GeoJSON and as CAP 1.2 in Atom (the format emergency managers' tools read), and storms as GeoJSON. For research: explore the database in your browser with Datasette Lite (people's readings, storm reports, events, storms and alerts for 90 days; people as handles with ZIP and county only), or download network.sqlite.

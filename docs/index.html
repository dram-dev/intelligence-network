The network

A sensor network you join from your phone. Report what you see — a rain gauge, hail on the deck, a creek level, a corn stage, smoke — in a short common language. Neighbors and official stations check every reading; corroborated readings become events, raise your trust, and roll into a daily digest.

How trust works

        
Every reading is compared with readings of the same metric nearby, inside the metric's distance and time window: other people, weather stations, NWS storm reports and warnings, and NOAA's radar estimates of rain and hail (MRMS). Agreement corroborates both readings. A sensor's trust, kept separately for each topic, is

        
trust = (K·prior + agreements) / (K + agreements + disagreements)
K = 4 · prior = 0.5 · trusted ≥ 0.8 · half-life 180 days

        
Old outcomes fade. Radar counts half as much as an official report. Two people under one roof, or two who always agree, count as one witness. New sensors show "new" instead of a number that looks exact; after that, the board shades an 80% range. Nobody starts trusted; nobody is condemned by one odd reading.

Pull the network's own snapshot

      
The page is built from fourteen JSON files that anyone may fetch and reuse: data/network.json, map.json (the day's notable readings and the news tied to them), counties.json, events.json, alerts.json, storms.json, reports.json, gauges.json, activity.json, graph.json, leaderboard.json, digests.json, topics.json, sources.json. Same anonymisation as the page.

      
For other systems: the week's verified events as GeoJSON and as CAP 1.2 in Atom (the format emergency managers' tools read), and storms as GeoJSON. For research: explore the database in your browser with Datasette Lite (people's readings, storm reports, events, storms and alerts for 90 days; people as handles with ZIP and county only), or download network.sqlite.
